Planning a Hawaii destination wedding means managing two separate administrative requirements:

- A Hawaii marriage license issued by the Department of Health.
- A Wiki Permit, also called a DLNR Right-of-Entry permit, when your ceremony involves commercial activity on a state public beach.These requirements apply whether you are planning a large destination wedding, a small ceremony, or an intimate **Hawaii elopement package**.

The rules differ by location. Oahu, Maui, Kauai, and the Big Island each have unique venue, access, and logistics considerations. Zenju Weddings and Events coordinates weddings across all four islands, helping couples understand what applies before the ceremony date is confirmed.

## START WITH THE RIGHT TIMELINE

### 12–18 MONTHS OUT: SECURE THE VENUE AND DATE

Popular Hawaii venues, private estates, resorts, and oceanfront locations may book 12–18 months in advance. Begin with:

- Island selection.
- Guest count.
- Venue or beach preference.
- Ceremony date.
- Reception requirements.
- Vendor availability.
- Travel and accommodation planning.A permit cannot solve an unavailable venue. Confirm the location and date first, then determine the applicable land-use requirements.

### SEVERAL WEEKS OUT: FINALIZE PERMITS

Wiki Permit timing depends on the applicant, location, date, and supporting documentation. Apply as early as possible once the ceremony details are established. Professional vendors commonly recommend allowing several weeks for processing, especially during peak travel and wedding seasons.

Your permit application may require:

- Ceremony date.
- Start and end time.
- Location.
- Guest count.
- Commercial activity details.
- Equipment or décor information.
- Certificate of Liability Insurance.The permit must be valid during the entire permitted activity, including any authorized setup and breakdown period.

## WHEN A WIKI PERMIT IS REQUIRED

The Hawaii Department of Land and Natural Resources, or DLNR, uses the Wiki Permits system for commercial activity on unencumbered state shorelines. Commercial activity generally includes a beach wedding, elopement, professional photography session, officiant services, or planning services provided for compensation.

A Wiki Permit is typically required when:

- Your ceremony takes place on a state public beach.
- A paid officiant, photographer, planner, videographer, or other professional is involved.
- The event uses the shoreline for commercial wedding activity.
- The location is listed as an eligible Wiki Permit beach.The [Hawaii DLNR Commercial Activities page](https://dlnr.hawaii.gov/ld/commercial-activities/) provides the current requirements and approved beach-location information. The [Hawaii.gov Wiki Permits service](https://portal.ehawaii.gov/home/online-services/wiki-permits/) also identifies beach weddings as a type of commercial activity requiring authorization.

A permit does not make the beach private. Public access remains in effect. Other beachgoers may be present, and couples cannot block the shoreline or claim exclusive use of the sand.

## COUNTY VS. STATE RULES: JURISDICTION MATTERS

A beach may appear to be part of a city or county park. That does not automatically mean the county controls the sandy shoreline.

The general jurisdictional rule is:

- Sandy shoreline: Usually regulated by the State of Hawaii through DLNR.
- Grass, pavilion, lawn, parking, or inland park area: May require authorization from the applicable county or park agency.
- Private estate, resort lawn, or private venue: May follow the venue’s own rules and may not require a state beach permit.DLNR states that, in most cases, the shoreline within a county park remains under state jurisdiction. Therefore, a county permit alone may not authorize a ceremony on the sand.

Additional county or venue approvals may apply if your event includes:

- A reserved pavilion.
- A lawn ceremony.
- A tent or reception setup.
- Parking or vehicle access.
- Amplified sound.
- A larger guest count.
- Exclusive use of a managed park area.Zenju reviews the ceremony footprint and location before recommending a permit pathway. This prevents a common destination-wedding mistake: assuming one permit covers every part of the event.

## COMMON PUBLIC-BEACH RESTRICTIONS

Every permit has specific conditions. Read the issued permit carefully. Requirements may vary by beach, island, agency, and event type.

Common restrictions for state public-beach ceremonies include:

- No large arches, arbors, tents, or backdrops.
- No chairs, tables, or structures unless expressly authorized.
- No amplified sound, generators, or powered equipment.
- No alcohol on the beach.
- No blocking public access.
- No throwing rice, glitter, confetti, or other materials into the sand.
- Complete removal of décor and personal items.
- Full compliance with leave-no-trace standards.A simple stand-up ceremony is generally easier to permit than a full production with furniture, installations, and a reception. This is one reason a beach **Hawaii elopement package** often has a streamlined design and a limited ceremony footprint.

The official Wiki Permit service lists a fee of **10 cents per square foot per event per day, with a $20 minimum**. Fees and conditions may change. Confirm the current amount through the official [Wiki Permits system](https://dlnr.ehawaii.gov/permits/welcome.html).

## WHAT IS NOT REQUIRED FOR A HAWAII MARRIAGE LICENSE

A beach permit and a marriage license are separate documents. Obtaining one does not replace the other.

For a Hawaii marriage license in 2026:

- No Hawaii residency is required.
- No U.S. citizenship is required.
- No blood test is required.
- No waiting period applies after issuance.
- Both applicants must appear together in person.
- Proxies are not permitted.
- Valid government-issued photo identification is required.
- The ceremony must take place within 30 days of license issuance.
- The current fee is $65, consisting of a $60 application fee and $5 portal fee.
- Fees are non-refundable.Apply online through the [Hawaii Department of Health marriage-license system](https://emrs.ehawaii.gov/emrs/public/home.html), then meet with an authorized agent no earlier than 30 days before the ceremony date.

The [Hawaii Department of Health marriage-license page](https://health.hawaii.gov/vitalrecords/marriage-licenses/) provides current information regarding agents, identification, prior marriages, appointments, and documentation.

The ceremony must be performed by a Hawaii-licensed marriage performer. Confirm that your officiant is authorized before signing a contract.

## ISLAND-BY-ISLAND COORDINATION

Permit and venue logistics depend on the island you choose.

- Oahu: Offers broad venue access, resort options, and convenient transportation. Public shoreline rules still apply, even near city or county parks.
- Maui: Combines resort properties, private venues, and popular public beaches. Confirm whether your ceremony is on private venue property or state shoreline.
- Kauai: Provides lush, scenic locations but may require more detailed transportation planning and weather contingencies.
- Big Island: Covers a large geographic area with varied landscapes, including resort coastlines, gardens, and volcanic terrain.
