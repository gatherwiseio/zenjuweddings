A Hawaii elopement can be small and simple. Planning one from the mainland still means coordinating flights, a legal marriage license, a ceremony location, and any permits that location requires. Start early, confirm the rules with the right agencies, and build a travel plan with room for changes.

For 2027, dates are filling unusually fast. Secure coordination early, especially if you have a specific island, location, or photographer in mind. Zenju Weddings and Events coordinates elopements on Oahu, Maui, Kauai, and the Big Island.

## STEP 1: CHOOSE YOUR ISLAND AND CEREMONY LOCATION

Start with the experience you want, not just a photo of a beach. Consider the setting, travel time, lodging, accessibility, and how much driving you and your guests are comfortable with.

![image](https://cdn.marblism.com/Qill0xbzeRA.webp)

- Oahu: A practical choice for many mainland travelers, with Honolulu as a major gateway and a broad range of lodging and vendor options.
- Maui: A fit for couples drawn to coastal and resort settings. Confirm how your chosen location, accommodations, and any vendor travel will work together.
- Kauai: A lush, nature-forward setting. Include extra time for island travel and check access and site requirements before choosing a location.
- Big Island: Offers varied landscapes and long travel distances. Choose a location with your airport, lodging, and ceremony-day transportation in mind.Once you’ve chosen an island, identify the exact ceremony location. “A beach on Maui” isn’t specific enough for planning: land ownership and jurisdiction determine which rules apply. A private venue, state shoreline, state park, and county facility can each have different requirements.

## STEP 2: APPLY FOR YOUR HAWAII MARRIAGE LICENSE

A marriage license is a legal requirement. It’s separate from a ceremony-site permit.

**For mainland couples, plan for an in-person appointment in Hawaii.** Both prospective spouses must appear together before an authorized marriage license agent. Proxies aren’t allowed. Complete the online application first, save your authorization or locator code, and bring valid government-issued photo IDs to the appointment.

Hawaii’s current Department of Health guidance states:

- No Hawaii residency or U.S. citizenship requirement applies.
- Blood tests aren’t required.
- There’s no waiting period after issuance.
- The license is valid only in Hawaii and expires 30 days from and including its issuance date.
- The current listed fee is $65: a $60 application fee plus a $5 portal fee. Check the official site for any changes before you apply.Schedule the appointment no earlier than 30 days before your ceremony. The ceremony must take place before the license expires. Because you’re traveling from the mainland, allow time to arrive, attend the appointment, and respond if travel plans change. Don’t leave the appointment until the last possible day.

Review the [Hawaii Department of Health marriage license requirements](https://health.hawaii.gov/vitalrecords/marriage-licenses/) and apply through the [Hawaii Electronic Marriage Registration System](https://emrs.ehawaii.gov/emrs/public/home.html). License fees and procedures may change; confirm current instructions for your travel date.

## STEP 3: CHECK PERMITS AND SITE RULES BEFORE BOOKING A PUBLIC BEACH

A public beach isn’t automatically an unrestricted wedding venue. The permit process depends on who manages the land and what you plan to do there. A small guest count doesn’t automatically exempt a ceremony, photography, or videography from the applicable rules.

![image](https://cdn.marblism.com/XaSWkGlulfG.webp)

Before you book, confirm the exact location’s jurisdiction and ask which approvals apply to your ceremony and vendors. The Hawaii Film Office notes that ceremony permits and photography permits can be separate. For eligible locations, an O+A ePermit covers photography or filming only; it does **not** authorize the ceremony itself. State shorelines may require a [DLNR Wiki Permits approval](https://wikipermits.ehawaii.gov/permits/welcome.html). State park weddings and elopements require a Special Use Permit, which the Film Office says must be submitted 45 days in advance.

Rules vary by location. For example, arches, chairs, décor, and other structures may be restricted or require specific approval. Don’t assume a permit for one activity covers everything. Review the [Hawaii Film Office’s elopement and wedding photography guidance](https://filmoffice.hawaii.gov/elopementwedding-photography/) and the [DLNR information on beach wedding permits](https://dlnr.hawaii.gov/ld/commercial-activities/). Confirm requirements directly with the relevant agency before making nonrefundable commitments.

## STEP 5: PLAN TRAVEL AND GUEST LOGISTICS

Your wedding day is only one part of a destination elopement. Build a travel plan that gives you breathing room.

- Arrive ahead of the ceremony. Allow time to settle in, attend your license appointment, and adjust if flights are delayed.
- Confirm lodging and transportation. Check the distance between your airport, accommodations, ceremony site, and any photo locations.
- Share clear guest details. Send the island, location, arrival time, parking or transport guidance, and any site-specific instructions.
- Plan for weather and access. Ask about a backup location or timing plan, and consider mobility, shade, and restroom access for you and your guests.
- Keep key documents together. Store your IDs, appointment details, vendor contacts, permit approvals, and travel information where you can access them.
